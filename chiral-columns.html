Chiral HPLC Columns

Our proprietary chiral stationary phases deliver superior enantioselectivity across a broad range of compound classes.

Key Highlights

Why Choose Cellachrom Chiral Columns?

Highest Resolution

We use high-quality filling material and unique packing technique to make columns. Our QC/QA-verified products achieve world-class resolution performance. High plate numbers result from size/shape uniformity of filling material, and asymmetry near 1 is due to our technique of column packing.

Long-Term Durability

Superior durability even in 100% THF conditions (immobilised columns). Designed for extended use in demanding pharmaceutical and research environments with consistent performance across many injections.

Exclusive CrownSil® Technology

Amino acid and peptide separation through CrownSil® columns — a unique 18-crown-6 acid CSP with methyl functional group available exclusively from CellaChrom. Specially designed for primary amines, aminoalcohols, and amines.

Chiral Column Types: Coated vs. Immobilised

Chiral columns are categorised by the method of chiral separation agent attachment to the silica support. Depending on the type, there are different types of chiral compounds that are well separated or not. The choice depends on your solvent system requirements and target compound class.

Category Description Key Feature Product Designation
Coated Column Chiral separation agent is coated on silica gel (physically adsorbed) High resolution but limited usable solvents (avoid THF, chlorinated solvents) A01A02
Immobilised Column Chiral separation agent is chemically bonded to silica gel Broad solvent compatibility including THF and chlorinated solvents; slightly lower resolution than coated B01B02

AmyloSil™

Amylose-based polysaccharide chiral stationary phase. Available as coated (A01, A02) and immobilised (B01, B02) types for maximum flexibility in solvent selection.

Amylose CSP
AmyloSil™-A01 & A02 (Coated Type):

Chiral separation agent coated on silica gel. Effective for separating racemates with halide, ether, ester, ketone, sulfoxide, alcohol, amine, acid, amide, and aminoalcohol functional groups. High resolution but limited compatible solvents.

AmyloSil™-B01 & B02 (Immobilised Type):

Chiral separation agent chemically bonded to silica. No limit on usable solvents — compatible with THF, chlorinated solvents, and all standard mobile phases. Suitable for compounds that require unusual solvent conditions.

CelluloSil®

Cellulose-based polysaccharide chiral stationary phase (2024). Available as coated (A01, A02) and immobilised (B01, B02) types. Complementary selectivity to AmyloSil columns.

Cellulose CSP
Chiral Selector: Cellulose tris-(3,5-dimethylphenylcarbamate) [A01/B01] and tris-(3-chloro-4-methylphenylcarbamate) [A02/B02]

Complementary enantioselectivity to amylose-based columns. Recommended for compounds that do not separate well on AmyloSil columns. Often used as the second column in a two-column screening strategy. The cellulose backbone provides a distinctly different chiral recognition environment.

Polysaccharide columns are separated as:

Coated (A-series): High resolution, limited solvent range • Immobilised (B-series): Full solvent compatibility including THF and DCM

CrownSil®

Advanced crown acid-based chiral stationary phase with unique methyl functional group (2023). Exclusive to CellaChrom. Specialized for amino acids, aminoalcohols, and amines.

Crown Ether CSP
Chiral Selector: 18-Crown-6 acid with methyl functional group (exclusive CellaChrom technology)

Specifically designed for separating racemates containing nitrogen functionality, including primary amines, amides, and aminoalcohols. The unique methyl group modification provides enhanced selectivity not available in conventional crown-ether columns. Operates in acidic aqueous mobile phases (perchloric acid or TFA at pH 1–2).

Polysaccharide columns compatible with Daicel:

AmyloSil™ and CelluloSil® amylose & cellulose-based columns are compatible with Daicel column conditions. CrownSil® columns are specialized for amino acids, aminoalcohols, and amines separation.
